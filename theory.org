* Governing Equations

The model describes steady-state, isothermal, incompressible flow of a generalized Newtonian fluid in a 2D domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$. The geometry is assumed invariant in the out-of-plane direction ($z$), and the velocity field has only one non-zero component:

\[
\mathbf{u}(x,y) = w(x,y)\,\mathbf{e}_z.
\]

Under this assumption, the problem reduces to a scalar shear flow problem in which pressure does not contribute to the in-plane momentum balance.

** Momentum Balance

Neglecting inertia and body forces, the reduced momentum balance becomes:

\[
\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{0},
\]

where the divergence is taken in the in-plane coordinates $(x,y)$ and $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ contains only shear components associated with the out-of-plane velocity gradient. For the present kinematics, this reduces to:

\[
\nabla \cdot \left( \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w)) \nabla w \right) = 0 \quad \text{in } \Omega.
\]

** Kinematics

The out-of-plane rate-of-deformation reduces to:

\[
\nabla w = \left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\right),
\]

and the equivalent shear rate is defined as:

\[
\dot{\gamma}(w) = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\right)^2}.
\]

** Constitutive Relation

The material is modeled as a generalized Newtonian fluid:

\[
\boldsymbol{\tau} = 2\mu(\dot{\gamma}) \mathbf{D},
\]

with viscosity according to the Cross model:

\[
\mu(\dot{\gamma}, T) = \eta_\infty(T) + \frac{\eta_0(T) - \eta_\infty(T)}{1 + (\lambda(T)\dot{\gamma})^{1-n}}.
\]

* Weak Form

To derive the weak form, we multiply the governing equation by a test function $v \in V_0$ and integrate over the domain:

\[
\int_\Omega \nabla \cdot \left( \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w)) \nabla w \right) v \, d\Omega = 0.
\]

Applying integration by parts yields:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
- \int_\Omega \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w)) \nabla w \cdot \nabla v \, d\Omega
+ \int_{\partial \Omega} \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w)) (\nabla w \cdot \mathbf{n}) v \, d\Gamma
= 0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Restricting the boundary integral to Neumann boundaries $\partial \Omega_N$ and rearranging gives:

\[
\int_\Omega \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w)) \nabla w \cdot \nabla v \, d\Omega
=
\int_{\partial \Omega_N} t_z \, v \, d\Gamma
\quad \forall v \in V_0,
\]

where:

- $t_z = \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w)) (\nabla w \cdot \mathbf{n})$ is the out-of-plane traction. In the present kinematics, the only non-zero stress components are shear stresses $\tau_{xz}$ and $\tau_{yz}$, which reduce to a scalar diffusion form.
- $V$ is the space of admissible trial functions satisfying Dirichlet conditions.
- $V_0$ is the corresponding test space with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions.

* Consistent Boundary Reactions

For Dirichlet boundaries, reaction forces are computed consistently from the discrete weak form.

Let $\Gamma_i$ be a Dirichlet boundary segment. The reaction force is obtained by evaluating the weak form residual using a test function $w_i \in V_0$ associated with that boundary:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
R_i &=
- \int_\Omega \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w_h)) \nabla w_h \cdot \nabla w_i \, d\Omega \\
&\quad + \int_{\partial \Omega_N} t_z \, w_i \, d\Gamma.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

If no external tractions are applied, this reduces to:

\begin{equation*}
R_i =
- \int_\Omega \mu(\dot{\gamma}(w_h)) \nabla w_h \cdot \nabla w_i \, d\Omega.
\end{equation*}

Here:
- $w_h$ is the finite element solution
- $w_i$ is the finite element test function associated with a unit value of the corresponding Dirichlet degree of freedom on $\Gamma_i$.

** Remarks

- This definition is consistent with the discrete weak form and ensures exact equilibrium up to solver tolerance
- It is more accurate than direct evaluation of boundary stresses
- The formulation is nonlinear due to the viscosity dependence on $\dot{\gamma}(w)$
- The governing equation reduces to a nonlinear diffusion problem for the out-of-plane velocity
